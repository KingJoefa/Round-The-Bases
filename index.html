Why does the banana win?

Try running super fast and then turning sharp, like the corner of a square. Your feet slide! You have to slow way down, turn, then speed up again.

A curve is a gentle turn. Bea leans in and keeps running fast the whole time. She runs a little more ground than Sam, but she never has to stop.

But if you only need first base, a straight line is the shortest and fastest way there. Ray never turns, so he never slows down before the bag. He hits it at full speed and stops after it. He's the first one to first base!

Second base is different. If you run past it, you can be tagged out. Sid runs fast almost all the way, then slides. Sliding on the dirt slows him down faster than his feet could, so he stops right on the bag.

Read the footprints

Each runner leaves a footprint every third of a second.

Look at Sam's footprints at each base. They bunch up. That's him putting on the brakes! Ray's footprints only bunch up after first base, once he's already safe. Sid has no footprints at the end, just a long skid mark in the dirt from his slide.

Coach's tips

Run a banana

When the ball goes to the outfield, swing out a few steps into foul territory before first base. Then curve back in toward the bag. A few steps is plenty. Too wide wastes steps (ask Wide Wally).

Tap the inside corner

Step on the corner of the base that points at the pitcher's mound. Push off it like a springboard toward the next base.

Don't chop your steps

Keep running in your normal stride. No tiny stutter steps to hit the bag. Lean into the turn like a bike.

Eyes up

Look at the base you are running to, not at the ball. After you touch a base, lift your head and find your coach.

Ground ball? Run straight!

If the ball stays in the infield, don't curve. Run through first base as fast as you can, like crossing a finish line.

Slide into 2nd and 3rd

You can run through first base, but not second or third. Start your slide about 9 big feet before the bag. Go feet first, and let the dirt stop you right on the base.

Listen to your base coaches

The 1st-base coach helps you until you reach 2nd. Then look to the 3rd-base coach. Go when they say go, stop when they say stop. And touch every base, or it doesn't count!

Practice game: Penguin Feet

A grown-up stands on the base with heels together, toes out, like a penguin. Their feet cover the bag except the inside corner. Run your banana and tap the only corner you can see!

For grown-ups: runners use a simple physics model. Every runner has the same top speed (19 ft/s) and the same grip. Sharp turns force a lower speed (speed limit grows with turn radius), so the square path brakes to about a quarter speed at each bag. Sid starts his slide 9 ft out and the dirt brakes him harder than running feet can (19 vs 14 ft/s²), so he keeps top speed longer before stopping on the bag. Tips come from youth coaching guides: Texas Rangers Youth Academy, Fatherly (coach Don Simon), MOJO, Veo, Dugout Captain, Baseball Canada, ACTIVE (sliding fundamentals).
